        An intervention can look straightforward in a protocol and become difficult to deliver on a prison wing or a busy intensive care unit. My work as a nurse academic connects institutional ethnography, critical posthumanism, workforce wellbeing and evaluation. These examples show what I contribute to collaborative research and practical projects, through university employment and volunteering.

        ## APPRAISE: alcohol-reduction support for men on remand

        APPRAISE asked whether alcohol-reduction support could be delivered and evaluated with men on remand, including after release. This NIHR-funded university collaboration worked in prisons in Scotland and England; its final report appeared in November 2024.

        Working with the University of Edinburgh team, I made substantial contributions to the study's conception and design, developed the protocol with colleagues, contributed to intervention training and data acquisition, and helped draft the report. My work connected research development with the practical requirements of delivering an intervention in prison. The team recruited 132 men, but delivery after release and follow-up proved difficult. The study identified constraints on continuity of support and the resources needed for further research. These are feasibility findings; they do not demonstrate that the intervention reduced drinking.

        [APPRAISE final report and contribution statement](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/107914/) · [Study protocol summary](/publications/appraise-study-protocol/)

        ## SHORTER: understanding implementation in critical care

        SHORTER compares a five-day antibiotic course with usual-care duration for critically ill adults with sepsis. I work at Edinburgh Napier University as **Senior Research Fellow (Process Evaluation)**, alongside **Process Evaluation Lead Dr Gosha Colquhoun**, within this ongoing UK NHS trial.

        I contribute to study design and the refinement and implementation of the protocol, and co-authored the published trial protocol in 2026. My process-evaluation work examines how the intervention is understood and delivered in clinical settings. The team's mixed-methods evaluation considers delivery, context and fidelity: how closely practice follows the intended intervention, and what shapes departures from it. The evaluation is designed to help interpret the trial and inform future implementation decisions.

        [SHORTER team and roles](https://www.shortertrial.com/our-team) · [Published trial protocol](/publications/shorter-trial-protocol/)

        ## PflegeStressPlan: nursing stress and working conditions

        At Charité I lead **PflegeStressPlan**, a continuing programme asking what nursing stress reveals about working conditions and the organisation of care. I bring evidence synthesis, quantitative measurement and qualitative inquiry together to examine how nursing work is organised and experienced. Diary and interview work continues alongside the measurement research.

        For our 2026 nursing-stress measurement paper, I developed the methods, undertook the statistical analysis and led the writing with colleagues. Using responses from 440 nursing and midwifery staff in one hospital, we examined a 57-item German instrument, including questions that did not apply to some respondents' work. The paper gives provisional support for use in comparable samples, while finding limited support for treating most subscales as separate outcomes. Its practical contribution is to clarify what scores can support before they are used to compare staff or services. The programme extends beyond this paper to the organisational conditions those measurements describe.

        [Programme leadership and research at Charité](https://gender.charite.de/en/metas/person/person/address_detail/msc_jamie_b_smith/) · [Measurement paper and findings](/publications/nursing-stress-measurement/)

        ## New Nurse Educator: evidence for educational guidance

        What continuing development do nurse educators need, and how should education respond? I contributed through the University of Edinburgh to **A New Agenda for Nurse Educator Education in Europe**, the 2020–2023 Erasmus+ partnership.

        As first author of its 2023 integrative review, I synthesised evidence with an international team on nurse educators' continuing professional development. We identified needs extending beyond teaching competencies to resources, management, collaboration and professional agency. The authors of the partnership's *Empowering Future Nurse Educators* handbook reproduce our model and use the findings in their discussion and recommendations on continuing development, occupational wellbeing and international collaboration. Our review contributed directly to that educational guidance.

        [Review summary and citation](/publications/nurse-educator-development/) · [Empowering Future Nurse Educators handbook](https://new-nurse-educator.utu.fi/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Empowering-Future-Nurse-Educators-Handbook-for-nurse-educators2023.pdf)

        ## Community and creative projects

        ### Rivelin Valley Allotments Society

        My voluntary work with Sheffield's **RVAS** combines community funding, accessible growing and digital resources for members. I developed a funding case for a ramp, accessible raised beds and improved shared facilities, drawing on members' assessment of their needs. I built the Society's website, turning its records into joining, volunteering, history and development pages, and developed online tool-induction forms and printable safety guidance.

        The Society received **£15,890 National Lottery funding in June 2026** for “Improving accessibility and longevity at the allotments”. Members continue to develop the shared space, with further access improvements planned.

        [Access project and continuing plans](https://rvas.co.uk/developments/) · [Tool library and member induction](https://rvas.co.uk/tool-library/)

        ### Steel City Beer & Cider Festival and Blue Bee

        For Sheffield's 2026 festival, I coordinated a shared Facebook and Instagram content calendar as a volunteer, covering the programme, brewers, sponsors and practical visitor information. I shaped the timing of posts, adapted copy and artwork for each channel, and handled scheduling, accessibility and corrections as plans changed. I also developed and edited a subtitled staff-induction film, bringing together volunteer recordings, demonstration footage, narration and festival graphics.

        A separate creative collaboration took my homebrew recipe, **A Cup of Ambition**, into commercial production: it was selected among the competition winners and brewed by **Blue Bee** as a New Zealand pale ale for the festival.

        [Festival and volunteer setting](https://sheffield.camra.org.uk/scbf/) · [Homebrew competition and brewing collaborators](https://sheffield.camra.org.uk/2026/10/steel-city-home-brew-competition/)
